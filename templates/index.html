    How it works

    Most models give you one answer and no sense of how much to trust it. Calibre uses split conformal prediction, a method from statistics that fixes this without assuming anything about the model.

    Your rows are split three ways: half to train a random forest, a quarter to calibrate, and a quarter held back as a final exam. On the calibration rows it measures how wrong the model tends to be, and uses that to size the ranges (for numbers) or the lists of possible classes (for categories). The exam rows, which nothing has seen, then check whether the promise held.

    The guarantee needs one thing: the data you predict on should look like the data you calibrated on. If your world changes, re-run it.

    Reference: Angelopoulos and Bates, A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification (2021).
